=== Probability of data loss after one disk has failed ===
Assumptions: annual failure rate per disk AFR = 2%; rebuild window = 24 h  -> P(one given disk fails during rebuild) q = 5.535e-05
RAID 1/10 (mirror partner must survive 1 disk): P(loss) = q                = 5.535e-05
RAID 5    (any of the other 5 disks fails)  : P(loss) = 1-(1-q)^5      = 2.767e-04  (5.0x worse than RAID1)
RAID 6    (two more failures needed)            : P(loss) ~ C(5,2)q^2       = 3.063e-08

Unrecoverable read errors during RAID5 rebuild (must read the 5 surviving disks = 20 TB):
  consumer HDD 1 in 1e14 bits         : P(at least one URE) = 79.8%
  enterprise HDD 1 in 1e15 bits       : P(at least one URE) = 14.8%
  enterprise SSD/near-line 1 in 1e16  : P(at least one URE) = 1.6%
  (RAID1/10 rebuild reads only 4 TB from one partner: with 1e-15 -> 3.1%; RAID6 can correct a URE during a single-disk rebuild)

=== Swap sizing (assumption: records server has 64 GB RAM, large in-memory database, no hibernation) ===
Rule of thumb for a server with 64 GB RAM and no hibernation: swap = 0.25 to 0.5 x RAM = 16 to 32 GB. Peak-load estimate: expected overflow 10 GB + 50% safety margin = 15 GB -> choose 16 GB (bottom of the range, because swap is only a safety net for a server sized to hold its working set in RAM).
Place it on the fast mirrored (RAID1/10) array, NOT on RAID5 (parity writes slow swap-out) and not on a single disk (a disk failure would crash processes that have pages in swap).
